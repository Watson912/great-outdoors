Trail Safety

Stay prepared, stay safe — every hike in the Pacific Northwest.

⚠️ Always check current trail conditions and weather forecasts before heading out. Conditions in the PNW can change rapidly.

Before You Go

🗺️

Plan Your Route

Study the trail map beforehand. Know your trailhead, key landmarks, turnaround point, and estimated return time.

🌦️

Check the Weather

Mountain weather in the PNW shifts fast. Check forecasts the morning of your hike and look for thunderstorm or wind advisories.

📣

Tell Someone

Leave a trip plan with a trusted contact — trail name, route, car description, and your expected return time.

🐻

Know the Wildlife

The PNW is home to black bears, cougars, and elk. Research proper wildlife encounters and carry bear spray on backcountry routes.

📱

Offline Maps

Cell service is unreliable in the mountains. Download offline maps (AllTrails, Gaia GPS) and consider a GPS device or satellite communicator.

🥾

Wear Proper Gear

Sturdy, broken-in hiking boots prevent ankle injuries. Dress in moisture-wicking layers and always carry a waterproof shell.

The Ten Essentials

🧭 Navigation (map & compass)
🌞 Sun protection (SPF & sunglasses)
🧥 Insulation (extra layers)
💡 Illumination (headlamp + batteries)
🩹 First-aid kit
🔥 Fire starter (matches & lighter)
🔧 Repair tools & knife
🍫 Nutrition (extra food)
💧 Hydration (water + filter)
⛺ Emergency shelter (bivy or tarp)

On the Trail

💧

Stay Hydrated

Drink before you feel thirsty. Aim for about half a litre per hour on moderate terrain. Filter all water from natural sources.

⏱️

Turnaround Times

Set a firm turnaround time — reaching the summit is optional, getting home safely is not. Most accidents happen on the descent.

⚡

Lightning Safety

Descend exposed ridges and summits immediately if storms build. Crouch low in a forest clearing and avoid lone trees or water.

🧊

Hypothermia Risk

Wet and windy conditions can cause hypothermia even in summer. Change out of wet clothes, eat, and seek shelter at the first signs of shivering.

🌿

Trail Etiquette

Uphill hikers have right of way. Yield to horses. Stay on marked trails to protect fragile alpine vegetation and prevent erosion.

🅿️

Leave No Trace

Pack out all trash, use established campsites, bury waste 200 ft from water, and leave wildflowers and rocks where you find them.

Emergency Information

🚨 Call for Help

In a life-threatening emergency, call:

911

For non-life-threatening mountain rescues in Washington, contact the county sheriff's office or call Washington State Patrol.

🩺 If Someone Is Injured

  • Keep the injured person warm and still
  • Send the strongest hiker for help with a written note of GPS coordinates
  • Mark your location on your phone (screenshot the coordinates)
  • Stay together if possible — never leave someone alone in the backcountry

🧭 If You Get Lost

  • Stop, stay calm, and stay put
  • Blow your whistle three times — the universal distress signal
  • Use your map and compass to retrace your steps
  • If after dark, set up your emergency shelter and signal with your headlamp
